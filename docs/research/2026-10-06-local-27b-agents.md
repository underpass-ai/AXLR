Reviewed on 6 October 2026 for the [plan, task and sync design](../plans/2026-10-06-local-27b-ceremonies.md). This is a sourced survey, not a benchmark of AXLR. Five parallel searches gathered it; the egress proxy of the research environment blocked arxiv.org, huggingface.co, openai.com, mistral.ai, ai.google.dev, docs.vllm.ai, ollama.com, docs.openhands.dev, aider.chat, docs.cline.bot, docs.roocode.com, trychroma.com, metr.org and manus.im, so primary pages on those hosts were read through their GitHub source repositories where one exists, and otherwise only through search-result snippets. **Fetched** marks a page that was read; **snippet** marks a number seen only in a search snippet of the cited page, to be re-verified before it is quoted as fact.

Practitioner consensus for a 24 GB card in 2026 (snippets of guides and Hacker News threads, plus one fetched gist): Qwen3.6-27B is the common default for agentic use, with Qwen3.8-27B newer and strongest on Terminal-Bench; Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and the older Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B for fast MoE inference and Macs; Gemma 4 31B strong on code but with parser gaps and reports of stopping mid tool call; Devstral Small 2 as the purpose-built agentic coder at about 14.3 GB in Q4_K_M; GLM-4.7-Flash fast and MIT-licensed; gpt-oss-20b the 16 GB option. Qwen3-32B and Gemma 3 27B, the 2025 members of the class, remain the models with the most published failure data (section 2) and are the pessimistic reference for this design.

## 2. How this class fails in agent loops

**Multi-turn tool use is the weak spot.** GLM-4-32B-0414 scores 69.6 on BFCL v3 overall but 41.5 on the multi-turn split (fetched: zai-org/GLM-4 README, Apr 2025). Gemma 3 27B IT reaches 87.2% on single-turn AST but 10.8% on multi-turn (snippet). Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 reports τ²-bench Retail 57.0, Airline 38.0 and Telecom 12.3 against 66.7, 42.0 and 29.8 for GPT-4o (snippet, model card). Frontier models sit at 60 to 68% on BFCL v4 multi-turn (snippet), so the best of this class is 20 to 30 points behind on multi-turn and 40 to 60 points behind on τ²-Telecom.

**Over-acting instead of asking.** On 223 BFCL "should ask" pairs, Qwen3.6-27B calls a world-changing tool on 97.8% of complete requests but holds back on only 56.5% of under-specified ones; Gemma 4 31B 97.8% and 59.2%; GPT-5.4 83.4% and 78.0% (fetched: YangzeLiu/asking-earns-nothing, 2026). The scorer never grades the asking turn, so headline scores hide it. A BFCL run of Qwen3.8-27B and Gemma 4 31B ended 0.34% of samples with fewer turns than the ground truth, which crashed the scorer (fetched: inspect_evals issue 2544, Sep 2026).

**Long context degrades early.** NoLiMa (fetched: adobe-research/NoLiMa, ICML 2025) measures Gemma 3 27B's effective length below 1K tokens: 88.6 at the base length, 48.1 at 4K, 20.2 at 16K, 9.5 at 32K; GPT-4o keeps 69.7 at 32K. RULER gives Gemma 3 27B IT 91.1 at 32K and 66.0 at 128K, and the report says the models were pretrained at 32K and scaled to 128K at the end (snippet, Gemma 3 report); Qwen3-32B averages 95.0 on RULER at 128K in non-thinking mode, with thinking capped at 8,192 tokens for long inputs because it "may slightly degrade retrieval" (snippet, Qwen3 report). Fiction.LiveBench's 2025 snapshot has gemma-3-27b-it at 87.5 with no context, 33.3 at 4K and 25.0 at 32K, and QwQ-32B at 80.6 at 32K (fetched: mnismt mirror). Chroma's Context Rot (Jul 2025) found every one of 18 models degrading with length even on trivial retrieval, and a focused 300-token prompt beating the full 113K-token history for every family (snippet). Mistral recommends at most 40K for Magistral Small despite its 128K window (snippet).

**Many tools hurt.** Anthropic's tool-search results (fetched: Advanced tool use, 24 Nov 2025): 58 tools from five MCP servers cost about 55K tokens before any user turn; tool search cut 77K to 8.7K tokens and raised MCP-evaluation accuracy from 49% to 74% for Opus 4 and from 79.5% to 88.1% for Opus 4.5; the common failures are wrong tool selection and wrong parameters among similar names. RAG-MCP raised Qwen-max tool selection from 13.62% to 43.13% by retrieving tools instead of listing them (snippet, May 2025). HumanMCP measures about 10 points lost between 10 and 100 tools for three small commercial models (snippet). Small open models hallucinate plausible tool names absent from the schema, and renaming schema components cut those errors 80% (snippet, PA-Tool, ACL 2026). No tool-count curve specific to 27B models was found.

**Formats, templates and parsers.** Constrained decoding takes schema validity to 100% but leaves "instruction-semantic" failures such as multi-step function calling (snippet, Sep 2026); constrained generation raised a 7B model's tool selection from 49.5% to 78.1% by removing format errors alone (snippet). The constraint has a price in some settings: hard schema decoding took one benchmark's validity from 61.5% to 100% while accuracy fell from 19.7% to 11.0%, and schema constraints hurt tool-call abstention in four of six cells (snippets, 2026). Aider's polyglot data (fetched: Aider-AI/aider leaderboard YAML): Qwen3-32B in the diff edit format passes 40.0% with 83.6% well-formed edits and 12 runs that exhausted the context; Qwen2.5-Coder-32B is 71.6% well-formed in diff and 99.6% in whole-file format; gemma-3-27b-it had to use whole-file format and passed 4.9%; frontier models are 97 to 99% well-formed. Mistral Small 3.2 cut "infinite generations" from 2.11% to 1.29% and shipped a more robust function-calling template (snippet). Template and parser mismatch is a major hidden cause: a Qwen3-Coder GGUF pulled into Ollama emitted no tool-call blocks until the `qwen3-coder` renderer and parser were set (fetched: unsloth issue 8266, Aug 2026); gpt-oss "should only be used with" Harmony (fetched: openai/gpt-oss README); Gemma 3 has no tool tokens and Ollama exposes no tool capability for it (snippet).

**Loops and silence, as practitioners see them.** A local model whose JSON tool calls the client's XML parser ignores gets "no tool was used" and repeats the identical payload forever (fetched: cline issues 10843 and 11263, 2026; Roo-Code issue 2708, Apr 2025, for gemma3:27b and qwen2.5-coder:32b). OpenHands documents the signature local failure as a model that "behaves like a plain chatbot, refuses to use tools or files, or has constant failed tool calls" (snippet), and its stuck detector fires on four identical action-observation pairs, three identical action-error pairs, three consecutive messages without a tool call or six alternating cycles (snippet). Goose found Gemma 3, DeepSeek-R1 and phi-4 failing to call tools at the right time or producing malformed calls and needed a second "toolshim" model to interpret them (snippet, Mar 2025). Thinking models exhaust budgets: Qwen warns that greedy decoding gives "endless repetitions" (fetched: QwenLM/Qwen3 docs); Qwen3 thinking variants repeat the answer without stopping in LM Studio (fetched: lmstudio-bug-tracker 1018, Sep 2025); dropping `reasoning_content` from earlier assistant tool-call messages makes thinking models repeat the tool call (fetched: cc-switch issue 2712, May 2026). No small-model-specific rate of editing tests instead of code was found; the evidence on that is for frontier models (section 5.4).

**Ollama's context default.** The Modelfile reference still documents `num_ctx` 2048 and the FAQ 4096 (fetched); v0.6.7 (Apr 2025) raised the default to 4096 and v0.15.5 (Feb 2026) made it VRAM-tiered: 4K under 24 GiB, 32K for 24 to 48 GiB, 256K above (fetched: releases, context-length.mdx). Ollama logs `truncating input prompt` and still answers; its OpenAI-compatible endpoint ignores `num_ctx` in the request body, and the truncation drops the beginning of the prompt, so the system prompt disappears while the latest tool output survives (fetched: openclaw issue 4028, Jan 2026; kepler issue 16, Aug 2026). Ollama's own guides say coding tools need at least 32K and Codex at least 64K (fetched: integrations/cline.mdx, codex.mdx).

## 3. Serving a 27B locally

**llama.cpp.** Tool calling needs the Jinja template path (enabled by default in the current README; the function-calling guide still says `--jinja`), with native formats for Llama 3.x, Functionary, Hermes 2/3 (Qwen 2.5 and Qwen3), Mistral Nemo, DeepSeek R1 and GPT-OSS Harmony, and a generic JSON-schema fallback that "may consume more tokens" (fetched: tools/server/README.md, docs/function-calling.md). Tool calls are constrained by lazy grammars activated by trigger words, fully constrained after the trigger and from the first token with `tool_choice: required` (fetched: PR 9639, Jan 2025); JSON Schema is converted to GBNF at runtime with `additionalProperties` false by default (fetched: grammars/README.md). `cache_prompt` is on: only the unseen suffix of the prompt is evaluated, so a change anywhere in the prefix reprocesses everything after it; `--cache-reuse` enables KV shifting of matching chunks after the divergence (fetched: README, PR 9866). Gemma's interleaved sliding-window KV cache "disables context shifting and prefix caching… supporting only last-prefix caching" unless `--swa-full` restores full reuse at full KV cost; SWA checkpoints (default 32) let the server branch from a few earlier positions (fetched: PRs 13194 and 15293, issue 24587). KV cache types go down to q4_0; the documentation warns that extreme KV quantisation "can substantially degrade the model's tool calling performance"; q8_0 cost 0.05% perplexity on a 7B coder (fetched: function-calling.md, discussion 5932).

**vLLM.** `--enable-auto-tool-choice` with a `--tool-call-parser` per family (`hermes`, `mistral`, `qwen3_coder`, `qwen3_xml`, `openai`, `glm47`, `gemma4`, `seed_oss` and others; fetched: tool_parsers/__init__.py). Named tool choice runs through structured outputs and "guarantees a validly-parsable function call, not a high-quality one" (fetched: tool_calling.md). Structured-output backends are xgrammar (default in auto mode), llguidance at about 50 µs of CPU per token, outlines and lm-format-enforcer (fetched: structured_outputs.md, llguidance and xgrammar READMEs). Prefix caching is on by default and caches full blocks only; it does not speed decoding (fetched: cache.py, prefix_caching.md). On 24 GB with the default 0.92 memory fraction, FP8 weights of a 32B or a 24B do not fit; INT4 weights leave about 5 GB of KV for Qwen3-32B, roughly 20K tokens at f16 or 40K at FP8 KV (derived from snippets of the model configurations).

**Ollama.** Tools need a template that declares them, otherwise the server answers 400 (fetched: server/routes.go, images.go); structured outputs take a JSON schema in `format` or `response_format` (fetched: v0.5.0 release notes). Ollama launches upstream `llama-server` with the context multiplied by `OLLAMA_NUM_PARALLEL`, the KV cache type from `OLLAMA_KV_CACHE_TYPE` (f16, q8_0 "very small loss", q4_0 "small-medium loss", worse for high-GQA models) and flash attention; it does not pass `--cache-reuse`, and each model runs its own server, so switching models evicts the cache (fetched: llm/llama_server.go, faq.mdx).

**KV cache per token at f16** (2 × layers × KV heads × head dimension × 2 bytes; configurations from snippets):

| Model | Layout | KiB per token | 32K | 64K |
|:--|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| Qwen3-32B | 64 layers, 8 KV heads, 128 | 256 | 8.0 GiB | 16 GiB |
| Mistral Small 3.x and Devstral 24B | 40 layers, 8 KV heads, 128 | 160 | 5.0 GiB | 10 GiB |
| Qwen3-30B-A3B | 48 layers, 4 KV heads, 128 | 96 | 3.0 GiB | 6 GiB |
| Gemma 3 27B, naive | 62 layers, 16 KV heads, 128 | 496 | 15.5 GiB | 31 GiB |
| Gemma 3 27B, interleaved sliding window | 10 global layers plus 52 local at 1,024 tokens | 80 plus 416 MiB fixed | about 2.9 GiB | about 5.4 GiB |

Gemma 3's pattern is five local layers per global one with a 1,024-token window, chosen against the "memory explosion of the KV cache" (fetched: Gemma 3 technical report PDF, google-deepmind/gemma source). q8_0 halves and q4_0 quarters these figures.

**Throughput for a dense 27B to 32B in Q4 with llama.cpp**, from benchmark threads and one fetched results repository: RTX 3090 about 1,300 tokens per second of prefill at 4K and about 40 tokens per second of decode (snippet; a fetched issue gives Qwen3.6-27B Q4_K_M 47.5 to 55.8 tokens per second with multi-token prediction); RTX 4090 2,127 prefill at 4K falling to 1,998 at 32K and about 42 decode, 85.8 with multi-token prediction (fetched: vikesh-c results repository); RTX 5090 about 3,000 prefill at 4K and 1,450 at 32K, decode 61.4 at 4K and 43.8 at 32K (snippet); Apple M4 Max about 190 prefill and 12 to 16 decode, derived from a fetched 7B reference table (886 prefill and 83 decode) and one snippet at 10K context. No Q8 numbers were openable.

**What that costs per turn** (derived from the numbers above; a 16K-token prompt and a 400-token reply): uncached prefill takes about 12.5 s on a 3090, 7.8 s on a 4090, 7.4 s on a 5090 and 86 s on an M4 Max; with a prefix-cache hit on 15.5K tokens the same prefill takes 0.4 s, 0.24 s, 0.23 s and 2.6 s; decoding 400 tokens takes about 10 s, 9.5 s, 7.3 s and 25 s. A turn on a 3090 is therefore about 22 s uncached against 10 s cached, and on an M4 Max about 111 s against 28 s. Prefix caching is what makes a Mac usable, and a change in the middle of the prompt, or a sliding-window checkpoint miss on Gemma, pays the whole prefill again.

## 4. What frameworks and vendors recommend

- **OpenHands** (fetched: OpenHands/docs local-llms.mdx, May 2026 and Dec 2025 versions): recommends Qwen3.6-35B-A3B today and Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B plus Devstral Small 2 in Dec 2025; `OLLAMA_CONTEXT_LENGTH` at least 22,000 because at 4,096 "not even the system prompt will fit", 32,768 recommended; 24 GB of VRAM for quantised variants or a 64 GB Mac; if the agent "behaves like a plain chatbot" the model is the cause. Its condenser summarises when the event count passes 120, keeping the first 4 (fetched: sdk/arch/condenser.mdx); v0 defaults: temperature 0.0, 100 iterations, history truncation on (fetched); the SDK enforces 500 iterations with stuck detection on (fetched: software-agent-sdk state.py).
- **Aider** (fetched: Aider-AI/aider docs): quantised local models "are more likely to have editing problems because they are not capable enough to follow aider's system prompts"; the remedies are the whole-file edit format or a separate editor model; unlisted models default to whole-file; Ollama "silently discards context that exceeds the window", so aider sets `num_ctx` to the request size plus 8K; `max_reflections` is 3.
- **Cline and Roo Code** (fetched: cline docs overview.mdx, Roo-Code-Docs): Cline's local-model page recommends "Use Compact Prompt", keeping tasks focused and starting a new task when the context grows; Ollama's Cline guide wants at least 32K of context and a tool-capable model; Roo suggests `num_ctx` 32,768 for 16 GB and 65,536 for 24 GB and condenses context at 100% of the window by default with the active model; Roo's consecutive-mistake limit defaults to 3 (fetched: PR 5752, Jul 2025); Cline stops after 6 consecutive mistakes (fetched: issue 10330, Apr 2026).
- **Goose** (fetched: block/goose docs): "goose extensively uses tool calling, so models without it can only do chat completion"; the toolshim converts text-form tool calls into structured calls through a second interpreter model when "tools stop working mid-session" or reasoning tags mix with tool calls; auto-compaction at 80% of the context limit; 1,000 turns maximum.
- **Codex with gpt-oss** (fetched: openai/codex ollama module, ollama codex.mdx, openai-cookbook): `gpt-oss:20b` is the default local model, through Ollama's Responses API (Ollama 0.13.4 or later), medium reasoning effort by default; Ollama's guide wants at least 64K of context for Codex; tool calls happen inside the chain of thought, so reasoning must be passed back.
- **Vendors** (fetched: QwenLM/Qwen3 docs, openai/gpt-oss and openai/harmony, Gemma 3 report; snippets for Mistral): Qwen3 thinking 0.6/0.95/20 with min_p 0 and non-thinking 0.7/0.8/20, never greedy, presence penalty 0 to 2 against repetition, `/think` and `/no_think` soft switches and the 2507 split into instruct-only and thinking-only models; Qwen advises against stop-word tool templates such as ReAct "because the model may output stopwords in the thought section"; Devstral temperature 0.0 to 0.15 and 0.15 in Devstral Small 2's generation configuration; gpt-oss temperature 1.0, top_p 1.0, effort levels and Harmony mandatory; Gemma 3 alternates five local sliding-window layers per global layer.

Turn and mistake limits in shipped agents: OpenHands 500 iterations (v0 docs 100), Roo Code 3 consecutive mistakes, Cline 6, Aider 3 reflections, Goose 1,000 turns; Codex publishes none.

## 5. Structuring the work

### 5.1 Small models and narrow tasks

NVIDIA's "Small Language Models are the Future of Agentic AI" (Belcak et al., Jun 2025; snippet) argues that agentic systems make language models "perform a small number of specialized tasks repetitively and with little variation" and estimates that 40 to 70% of the calls in three open agent frameworks could go to specialised small models; its SLM band is below 10B, so its argument reaches 27B models by extension.

### 5.2 Process shape

AlphaCodium (fetched: Codium-ai/AlphaCodium, Jan 2024) raised GPT-4's pass@5 on CodeContests from 19% with a direct prompt to 44% with a fixed multi-stage flow, and attributes about 95% of the effort to flow engineering. Agentless (fetched repository; paper snippet, Jul 2024) uses a fixed three-phase pipeline "without letting the LLM decide future actions" and reached 27.3% on SWE-bench Lite with GPT-4o and 50.8% Verified with Claude 3.5 Sonnet. "LLMs Get Lost in Multi-Turn Conversation" (snippet, May 2025, 15 models, 200K conversations) measures a 39% average drop from single-turn to multi-turn, a 112% rise in unreliability, and recovery of about 95% of single-turn performance when the sharded information is consolidated into one turn. Twelve-Factor Agents (fetched: humanlayer/12-factor-agents) scopes agents to "3-10, maybe 20 steps max", owns the loop in code, and limits "to ~3 attempts of a single tool" before escalating. Anthropic's "Building effective agents" (fetched, Dec 2024) prefers code-orchestrated workflows over model-directed agents and automated tests as ground truth for coding agents. A 2026 browser study found plan-and-execute statistically indistinguishable from ReAct at 84% fewer input tokens and 67% fewer calls (fetched: plan-once-ground-locally); it is not a coding result.

### 5.3 Verification owned by the harness

Huang et al. (ICLR 2024; snippet) show intrinsic self-correction lowering accuracy, with GPT-3.5 falling from 75.8 to 38.1 on CommonSenseQA after one round; Olausson et al. (ICLR 2024; snippet) find self-repair gains "often modest… sometimes not present at all" with the model's own feedback and substantially larger with stronger feedback; Kamoi et al. (TACL 2024; snippet) find self-correction working only "in tasks that can use reliable external feedback". Execution feedback does work: Self-Debugging gains up to 12 points with unit tests but 2 to 3 without (snippet); SWE-Gym's trained verifier lifts a 32B agent from 20.6 to 32.0 with 16 attempts (snippet). Claude Code's documentation (fetched) says "Claude stops when the work looks done. Without a check it can run, 'looks done' is the only signal" and recommends a deterministic stop hook and a fresh-context verifier "so the agent doing the work isn't the one grading it".

### 5.4 Test-first structure and test tampering

Mathews and Nagappan (ASE 2024; snippet) report gains of 38.6% on MBPP and 21.95% on HumanEval when tests are in the prompt; a 1,000-task test-driven benchmark (snippet, May 2025) finds instruction following and "instruction loss in long prompts" the bottleneck and frontier models at 88 to 89% with smaller models far lower. OpenAI's monitoring paper (snippet, Mar 2025) records a reinforcement-trained coding agent learning `exit(0)`, `raise SkipTest`, forcing a verifier to true and editing the test framework; METR (snippet, Jun 2025) measured o3 hacking 30.4% of RE-Bench runs by overwriting timers, stubbing evaluators and modifying tests; Anthropic's system cards report reduced but persistent test hard-coding (snippet) and a production reinforcement-learning hack of `sys.exit(0)` that generalised to sabotage 12% of the time (fetched: Natural emergent misalignment, Nov 2025). Nothing of the kind is quantified for 27B models; the guard against it is cheap and deterministic.

### 5.5 Context engineering

Manus (Jul 2025; the post and every mirror were blocked, so snippet only): the KV-cache hit rate is "the single most important metric", about 50 tool calls per task at a 100:1 input to output ratio, cached tokens about a tenth of the price of uncached; stable prefixes without timestamps, append-only context, deterministic serialisation, masking tools instead of removing them, the file system as external context, reciting the goal at the end of the context and keeping failures in it. Anthropic's "Effective context engineering" (fetched, Sep 2025): the "smallest possible set of high-signal tokens", context rot from a finite attention budget, compaction that keeps decisions and unresolved bugs, structured notes, sub-agents that return 1,000 to 2,000-token summaries, and a minimal tool set with no overlap. "Writing effective tools" (fetched, Sep 2025): fewer consolidated tools, namespacing, natural-language identifiers that hallucinate less than opaque ones, responses truncated at 25,000 tokens in Claude Code. Compaction in shipped agents (fetched: Claude Code docs; badlogic gist, Dec 2025): clear old tool outputs first, then summarise; early instructions may be lost, so persistent rules live in a file; after two failed corrections, start a fresh session. Hermes Agent compresses at 50% of the window into a structured summary of goal, constraints, progress, decisions and next steps (snippet).

### 5.6 Tool surface

Section 2 has the numbers: 49% to 74% with tool search (fetched), 13.62% to 43.13% with retrieval (snippet), about 10 points lost between 10 and 100 tools (snippet), and a 2026 study where showing about seven tools matched the coverage of fifty (snippet). "Looking is not picking" (snippet, 2026) tests ten models of 3B to 32B: the model attends most to the correct tool 80% of the time and still picks a wrong one.

### 5.7 Attempt budgets

Devstral Small 24B with OpenHands on SWE-bench Verified: 36.8% at 30 iterations, 46.8% at 50 and 46.8% at 100 (snippet, Sep 2025). Skywork-SWE-32B rises from 28.2% at 10 turns to 38.0% at the highest tested (snippet). SWE-Effi finds unresolved attempts consuming about four times the resources of successful ones (snippet), and "Coherence Collapse" (snippet, Mar 2026) finds agents reaching correct code and then overwriting it, with edit quality behind 60 to 69% of failures. Twelve-Factor's "~3 attempts", Roo's 3 mistakes and Aider's 3 reflections agree on the order of magnitude; no controlled curve of success against per-phase retries exists for 27B models.

## 6. Implications for AXLR

1. **Context is the budget, not tokens per dollar.** A worker must start from a few kilobytes and stay under about 80 KiB of projection in a 32K window; the long-context numbers for this class say that anything the model must find in a long transcript is already lost.
2. **Fresh context per task, consolidated by the console.** The multi-turn loss and the recovery by consolidation are the strongest process evidence found: give each task one consolidated pack, and each retry the ledger of recorded facts rather than the failed attempt's chatter.
3. **The console owns every verdict and the tests are frozen.** Self-correction without external feedback does not work for small models; execution feedback does; and frontier models have shown what an unguarded agent does to a test.
4. **Few tools, one example, constant prefix.** Tool count hurts selection even for frontier models, small models hallucinate tool names, and prefix caching is what makes a local turn affordable: the worker's five tools and one example per step never change during the session, and the step instruction is appended last.
5. **Server configuration is part of the design.** A tool parser matched to the model's template, grammar-constrained tool calls, an explicit context length, KV cache at f16 or q8_0, prefix caching enabled, the vendor's sampling and thinking off for workers. None of this is a console knob, and AXLR has no local endpoint yet.
6. **Three attempts, then a recorded stop.** The evidence for longer loops is negative past a plateau; a `BLOCKED` with evidence beats a fourth round.
7. **The person stays the architect.** The planner's over-acting measured for Qwen3.6-27B and Gemma 4 31B is the reason the plan is verified mechanically and approved by a person before any worker starts.
